Weekly Report: Why This Correction Is Not Another 2022

Stocks are breaking down after a false October breakout, driven by rising oil prices and a spike in bond market volatility. Because underlying corporate earnings and economic momentum remain strong, this pullback looks like a tactical flush on the way to a year-end buying opportunity rather than a 2022-style bear market. In this weekly report, we examine bond market volatility, forward earnings, and sector valuations, along with trend structures and our expectations for SPX, OIL, and SMH.

01

BOND VOLATILITY (MOVE)

Since early September, rising term premiums and 10Y yields have pushed bond market volatility (the MOVE index) sharply higher. Equities ignored the pressure at first, printed a false breakout in early October on both the S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100, and are now breaking below their 100-day moving averages.

Trapped buyers at the top typically create a liquidation flush on the way down. However, true capitulation is not here yet: we would need to see the VIX spike into the 28 to 35 range to confirm a blood-on-the-streets bottom.

03

NASDAQ 100 POSTS WORST MONTHLY LAG

The tech-heavy Nasdaq 100 just recorded its worst monthly underperformance relative to the equal-weighted S&P 500 since 2002. The monthly performance gap reached roughly 10 percentage points.

Unlike standard market-cap indexes dominated by a few giants, the equal-weighted S&P 500 gives every company the same weight. A gap of this size highlights a historic rotation out of mega-cap technology stocks and into the broader market.

While this shift shows that other sectors are holding up better than tech, the extreme speed of the move could also signal that selling in big tech is reaching a stretched level in the short term.

04

NASDAQ: PULLBACK DEPTH

The Nasdaq-100 (NDX) is down 10.42% from its recent peak. If the pattern from the last two corrections repeats, the index could find a local bottom this week.

The previous two pullbacks saw peak-to-trough declines of 12.70% and 11.66%. Both bottoms formed as the index briefly undercut its 200-day moving average (a key long-term trend line) and pushed the Relative Strength Index (RSI, a momentum indicator) into oversold territory near 29–32.

A similar drop toward roughly 12% alongside an oversold RSI reading could serve as one potential signal that selling pressure is exhausting.

05

OIL IS THE DOUBLE WILDCARD

Crude oil is the main catalyst to watch right now:
- Near-term risk: Further upside in oil keeps upward pressure on bond yields and practically guarantees a sharper equity flush.
- Structural risk: Only a massive 30-40% surge in oil would force the Fed into aggressive rate hikes and break the economic сycle.
- At current prices, oil has not dented corporate earnings over the past six months.

08

VALUATIONS ARE ALREADY AT CRISIS LOWS

Because bond volatility pulled risk off the table while earnings kept climbing, multiples across key sectors are already pricing in a recession that is not happening:
- S&P 500 Semiconductors: Forward P/E is at 14.4x, matching the 2020 (!) pandemic low and the 2022 bear market bottom. Unlike 2022, semi earnings today are surging, not contracting.
- Nvidia (NVDA): Forward P/E is down to 15x, its cheapest level in over a decade (lower than the 2018, 2020 and 2022 bottoms). For NVDA to drop another 30-40% from here, earnings would have to fall ~30% next year. Once the flush ends, multiple expansion alone can drive a major rally in the index.
- Small Caps (S&P 600): Trading at 14x forward P/E (on par with 2001, 2011, 2018 and 2020 lows) despite a multi-year melt-up in forward EPS. Even if the multiple slips to 13x for another 5-10% dip, the upside asymmetry is strong.

10

TECH POSITIONING VS. EARNINGS REALITY

Current investor positioning in mega-cap growth and tech stocks implies annual earnings growth of roughly 10%. That compares with actual blended earnings growth of 47% in Q3 2026 and consensus forecasts of 36% by the end of next year.

Since 2011, fund positioning has tracked tech earnings growth with a 54% correlation. Right now, a wide gap has opened between cautious investor exposure and actual profit numbers.

Market participants have positioned for a sharp earnings slump that has not yet appeared in financial results. If profit growth holds near consensus estimates, this light positioning could act as fuel for a rebound in tech shares.

11

S&P 500 Earnings Surprises Are Narrowing

S&P 500 companies are still beating profit estimates in the Q3 2026 earnings season, but by a much smaller margin than earlier this year. Twelve trading days into the season, the cumulative earnings-per-share (EPS) growth surprise stands near 2 percentage points.

That is well below the outsized beats seen in Q1 and Q2 2026, which finished between 7 and 9 percentage points above forecasts. The current pace is also tracking slightly below the historical average quarter since 2005, which typically levels off near 3.5 percentage points.

Corporate profits are still clearing the bar, just with smaller positive surprises. With companies delivering more modest beats, earnings reports may provide less immediate fuel for stock rallies.

12

MIDTERMS: A TAILWIND

According to JPMorgan, the S&P 500 has delivered positive returns after every US midterm election since 1990, averaging gains of 11.4%, 12.5%, and 14.7% over the following 6, 9, and 12 months. Betting markets currently price roughly a 67% probability of a Democratic sweep, compared to about 27% for a split Congress.

JPMorgan notes that recent de-risking has not yet reached full capitulation. Selling by algorithmic quant funds and crowded positioning in tech stocks still pose near-term risks.

At the same time, exposure to momentum stocks (recent market winners) has dropped to just 30% of its peak alongside broad hedge fund deleveraging (cutting borrowed positions). Once election uncertainty clears, these cleaner conditions could support a sharp rebound.
