Fear Peaks, Positioning Flushes: Is the Bottom Near?

Risk models hit extreme oversold, the Vol Panic Index logs its biggest two-day spike since 2007, and the Nasdaq loses a record $2.49 trillion in a single day. Hedge funds dumped AI stocks at the fastest pace in five years, pushing positioning to levels that price in zero earnings growth while real profits grow near 15%. Add $5 billion a day in returning buybacks and the cheapest Nasdaq-100 valuation since 2020, and the ingredients for a bottom are gathering. We map the key SPX levels that decide whether this is a reset or the start of a deeper correction. A detailed breakdown of these signals, plus our S&P 500 and SMH road map for the coming weeks, is in this week's report.

03

Vol Panic Index Spikes Back Into the Panic Zone

The Vol Panic Index jumped to 9.57 out of 10, pushing the gauge back into the panic zone (9.5 and above). This move marked the largest two-day surge in the indicator's history since tracking began in 2007.

Measured on a 0 to 10 scale, the index tracks extreme market stress and sudden demand for downside protection. At current levels, fear is nearly maxed out.

Such extreme readings show investors are rushing to hedge against further losses. While high volatility can persist in the very short term, panic at these levels often appears when a market sell-off is nearing exhaustion.

04

Nasdaq Loses Record $2.49 Trillion in One Day as AI Stocks Tumble

The Nasdaq Composite shed a record $2.49 trillion in market value on Thursday, November 5. For perspective, the entire Nasdaq market was worth $2.95 trillion at the end of 2003.

AI, semiconductor, and high-growth stocks suffered the sharpest weekly losses. Memory, optical networks, and inference chip baskets fell between 16% and 18% over five days, while the S&P 500 excluding AI slipped just 0.3%. Heavy selling by hedge funds and thinner market liquidity made the drop steeper.

With investor positioning still crowded in some areas and heavily washed out in others, the near-term direction for tech stocks remains uncertain heading into the new week.

05

Record Positioning Washout: How Funds Dumped Tech and Broad Equities

The U.S. sell-off triggered a historic positioning flush. Forced selling from leveraged ETFs hit a record $56.4 billion in one day, while hedge funds sold AI stocks at the fastest 20-day pace in over 5 years.

Per Deutsche Bank, megacap tech positioning collapsed to the 7th percentile of its historical range. That level prices in shrinking tech profits, even though actual Q3 sector earnings growth sits above 50%.

Across the broader market, weekly equity positioning suffered one of its biggest drops since 2020, pushing overall exposure underweight to the 20th percentile. Broad positioning now reflects zero earnings growth, far below current S&P 500 profit growth near 15%. This gap points to an emotional washout rather than a fundamental collapse.

06

Nasdaq-100 Valuation Drops to Its Lowest Level Since 2020

The forward P/E ratio (price-to-earnings over the next 12 months) for the Nasdaq-100 has fallen to 18.4x. That is its lowest reading since 2020 and well below both its 5-year and 10-year averages.

This drop means major tech stocks have become much cheaper relative to their expected profits. The scale of the valuation reset is now almost as striking as the recent fall in stock prices.

Cheaper valuations alone do not guarantee an immediate market bottom. Still, a reset to multi-year lows could start attracting long-term buyers looking for more reasonable entry levels in the tech sector.

07

S&P 500 Buybacks Ramp Up to $5 Billion a Day as Blackout Window Ends

Starting Monday, November 9, roughly 90% of S&P 500 companies have exited their earnings blackout period (the window before quarterly results when companies pause share repurchases). Corporate buyback demand is now running at around $5 billion per day.

According to the Goldman Sachs buyback desk, repurchase volumes picked up sharply in the second half of the week, with companies stepping in to place additional orders during Thursday's sell-off. Weekly buyback activity jumped to 1.4 times the 2025 average daily volume, up from just 0.6 times the prior week.

This steady return of corporate buying could provide a key source of support for U.S. stocks following the recent pullback.

08

Equity Positioning Drops to Underweight and Prices In Zero Earnings Growth

According to Deutsche Bank, consolidated U.S. equity positioning has plunged into underweight territory, falling to the 20th percentile of its historical range since 2010. Investors rapidly cut their stock exposure during the latest pullback.

At current levels, equity positioning is in line with zero corporate earnings growth. That stands in sharp contrast to actual fundamentals, as S&P 500 profits continue to expand at a healthy pace, with Q3 median growth running near 15% year-over-year.

This wide gap between cautious investor positioning and solid profit growth suggests the recent sell-off was driven more by fear than by a deterioration in business results. Once sentiment stabilizes, strong earnings could help draw buyers back in.
